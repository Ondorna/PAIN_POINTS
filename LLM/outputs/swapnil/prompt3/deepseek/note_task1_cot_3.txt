## Project update

**Project purpose:**  
The project is the “Ro-Mee-Bo (?)” robot, described as a therapeutic social robot for autistic children who are overwhelmed with stimulation. It is intended to let a therapist or instructor with a limited vocabulary drive and interact with the robot to engage a student with speech pathology. The broader effort also includes an open source robotics software stack and a kit-of-parts approach for the robotics group to play with and iterate on.

**Project lead:**  
Not explicitly stated in the meeting notes. Cory is the presenter discussing the project and says he recently joined the group, but no formal project lead is named.

**Current robot cost:**  
$700 price point.

**Target cost:**  
$150 price point.

**Technology stack:**  
Open source software stack for robotics being developed by Cory. Hardware noted includes an iPod Touch in the back, plus generic robot components described as “a pair of wheels, a battery, a brain, and a bunch of other stuff.” The notes do not specify exact languages, frameworks, boards, or other detailed stack components.

**Robotics group meeting day and time:**  
Not stated in the meeting notes. The notes mention a robotics meeting/group but give no day or time.

**Distribution/manufacturing plan:**  
The project wants to open source the robot rather than sell products. Cory’s plan involves a kit of parts that can be purchased, with manufacturers interested in manufacturing and the possibility of manufacturing in a makerspace. He proposes potentially selling a package at Noisebridge, manufactured there, with money going to Noisebridge for parts and materials. Trent suggests a workshop model where participants pay for the kit and work with someone knowledgeable, with sold items and proceeds donated to Noisebridge.
